Yes. Those white cylindrical pieces are generally called spacers or standoffs.

In DIY robotics and linkage mechanisms, they can go by several names depending on the material and where you buy them:

Plastic Spacer (most common name)
Nylon Spacer
Plastic Standoff
Spacer Sleeve
Tube Spacer
Nylon Bush (sometimes, though "bush" usually refers to a bearing-like part)

In your image, they're being used as spacers to keep the two popsicle sticks at a fixed distance apart, which:

Increases the arm's rigidity.
Prevents the sticks from twisting.
Makes the linkage much stronger without adding much weight.

They're simply screwed or bolted through the center.

If you want to buy them

Search for:

M3 Nylon Spacer
M3 Plastic Spacer
M3 Standoff
M3 Female-Female Nylon Standoff (if threaded)
M3 Unthreaded Nylon Spacer (if it's just a tube)

The "M3" refers to the screw diameter (3 mm), which is very common in robotics.

For your robotic arm project, I'd recommend using nylon spacers because they are:

✅ Lightweight
✅ Electrically non-conductive
✅ Cheap
✅ Strong enough for small robotic linkages

If you're building a more precise arm, you can also use aluminum standoffs for even greater stiffness.
